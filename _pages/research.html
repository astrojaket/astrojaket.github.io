Research

Exoplanet atmospheres and atmospheric inference

My work focuses on using JWST and complementary datasets to understand the composition, structure, and clouds of exoplanet atmospheres. I am particularly interested in how atmospheric signals change across planet size, temperature, and observing geometry.

Gas giants

Hot Jupiters and warm gas giants are strong laboratory targets because their large atmospheric signals reveal chemistry, temperature structure, and cloud properties with comparatively high precision.

Terrestrial planets

Smaller planets around nearby stars are central to the search for atmospheric diversity and, ultimately, habitability. I work on methods that separate planetary signals from stellar contamination.

Model comparison

I use retrieval frameworks and forward models to test which atmospheric assumptions are actually supported by the data, including cloud models and instrument-specific effects.

Selected themes

JWST-era atmospheric studies

I led one of the first atmospheric analyses of a hot Jupiter using JWST observations and continue to work on spectra that probe temperature gradients, clouds, and molecular abundances.

TRAPPIST-1 and rocky worlds

A major focus of the field is determining how stellar contamination and observational systematics affect the interpretation of terrestrial-planet spectra. I work on these problems with a team based in Montréal and elsewhere.

Machine learning

I spent a year as a Schmidt AI in Science Fellow exploring machine learning methods that can be applied to exoplanet atmosphere analysis. That work remains an important part of my broader research toolkit.
